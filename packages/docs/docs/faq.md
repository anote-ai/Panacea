# Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions we hear most from teams evaluating Anote for document Q&A — privacy, accuracy, and what to do when the AI gets it wrong.

---

## Is my data private? Is it used to train models?

**Your documents are never used to train models — not by Anote, and not by the LLM providers we use.**

Here is exactly where your data lives and what touches it:

| Data | Where it's stored |
|---|---|
| Uploaded files | Server disk (`UPLOAD_FOLDER`) on the deployment you use |
| Text chunks + embeddings | A local [ChromaDB](https://www.trychroma.com/) vector store on the same server |
| Document metadata, chats | The deployment's MySQL database, scoped to your account |

- **Embeddings are computed locally.** Chunking and embedding use ChromaDB's built-in embedding model running on the server — document text is not sent to a third-party embedding API.
- **Answer generation calls an LLM API.** When you ask a question, the question plus the small set of retrieved text chunks (not the whole document) are sent to the configured LLM provider — Anthropic by default; OpenAI or Google if you bring your own key. Both [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms) and [OpenAI](https://openai.com/policies/api-data-usage-policies) do not train on API data by default under their commercial API terms.
- **Access is scoped to your account.** Every document and chat endpoint requires authentication, and queries are filtered by user ID.
- **Deleting a document deletes everything derived from it**: the stored file, its thumbnail, its database record, and its embeddings in the vector store.

!!! tip "Need zero data egress?"
    The **desktop app (Private GPT)** runs the entire stack — backend, vector store, and, with [local models via Ollama](desktop/local-models.md), the LLM itself — on your own machine. In that configuration, nothing leaves your computer.

## How accurate are the answers?

Honest answer: **it depends, and the system can and does make mistakes.**

Accuracy is driven by three things:

1. **Document quality.** Clean, digital text (exported PDFs, Word documents, Markdown) works well. Scanned PDFs without a text layer currently extract little or no text — if a document indexes with zero or very few chunks, answers about it will be poor.
2. **Question specificity.** "What is the termination notice period in section 8?" retrieves far better context than "summarize the risks."
3. **The underlying model.** Answers are generated by frontier LLMs (Claude by default), which are strong at reading comprehension but are not infallible.

We do not yet publish a formal accuracy benchmark for the document Q&A pipeline; retrieval-quality work (query expansion, re-ranking, and per-answer confidence scores) is actively in progress ([#204](https://github.com/anote-ai/Panacea/issues/204)). Until then, treat every answer as a well-informed draft, not a verified fact.

## What happens when it's wrong?

Design assumption: **the system is a research assistant, not an authority.**

- **Grounded answering.** Answers are generated only from text retrieved out of *your* documents. If nothing relevant is found, the system says it could not find relevant information rather than inventing an answer from general knowledge.
- **Source citations are rolling out.** Showing the exact document, page, and text chunk behind every answer is in active development ([#197](https://github.com/anote-ai/Panacea/issues/197)), so you can verify any claim in one click.
- **Verify critical claims.** For legal, medical, or financial decisions, always confirm the answer against the source document before acting on it. The fastest workflow is to ask a specific question, then open the document to the cited passage and confirm the language yourself.

## What document types and languages are supported?

**File types:** PDF, DOCX, TXT, Markdown, and CSV. File type is validated at upload.

**Languages:** The answer-generating models (Claude, GPT) are strongly multilingual. Retrieval uses an English-optimized embedding model, so cross-language recall is best-effort: English documents with English questions give the best results today.

---

## What's the maximum document size and number of documents?

- There is no hard per-file size cap in the application; very large documents simply take longer to index. Documents are split into ~1,000-character chunks for retrieval, so answer quality does not degrade with document length — retrieval always works over the most relevant chunks.
- There is no cap on the number of stored documents. Folders let you organize documents into projects, and questions can be scoped to a single document or a folder.

## Can I use this with confidential documents?

Yes, with the deployment model matched to your sensitivity level:

- **Hosted:** documents are stored server-side, scoped to your account, and never used for training (see the privacy answer above).
- **Self-hosted:** run the full stack on your own infrastructure with `docker compose up` — your documents, database, and vector store never leave your network. Only the retrieved-chunk-plus-question LLM calls go out, to a provider you choose and key you own.
- **Fully offline:** the desktop app with Ollama local models keeps every byte on one machine — appropriate for the strictest confidentiality requirements.

We do not currently claim SOC 2 or HIPAA certification. For regulated data (PHI, privileged legal material), use the self-hosted or desktop deployment so the data stays under your existing compliance boundary.

## How does pricing work — am I charged per question or per document?

**Per question, via a monthly plan allowance — never per document.**

- Uploading and storing documents is free on every plan.
- Each paid plan includes a monthly question allowance (Basic: 750, Pro: 2,000, Enterprise: 6,000) plus a credit balance for advanced features; one-off credit top-ups are available without changing plans.
- Self-hosted deployments have no per-question charges from Anote at all — you pay only your own LLM provider for API usage.

See the in-app billing page for current prices, or the [payments API](api/overview.md) for programmatic access.
